# Risk Register

This document provides a comprehensive risk analysis for the Maternal Health Data Reliability Scorer, evaluating operational, technical, and clinical data risks in remote maternal monitoring.

In accordance with safety guidelines, qualitative risk descriptions are documented based on system architecture and code behavior without assigning unsupported numerical likelihood or severity probabilities.

---

## Comprehensive Risk Matrix

| # | Risk | Cause | Potential Impact | Detection Mechanism | Mitigation Strategy | Safe Fallback | Remaining Limitation |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **1** | **Device Gaps** | Multi-week hardware outage, battery exhaustion, damaged sensors, or patient travel without monitoring equipment. | Sparse longitudinal records; missed acute hypertensive onset or spurious trend estimation over sparse endpoints. | `is_gap` flag triggered when interval exceeds $2 \times \text{expected interval}$; missing rate calculation in `check_quality()`. | Completeness sub-score penalization; sample size $<5$ observations suppresses trend fitting. | Unconditional `SAFE_FALLBACK` (alert suppressed); care coordinator prompted to contact patient and request 3 consecutive daily readings. | Cannot retrospectively recover unrecorded physiological events that occurred during the gap. |
| **2** | **Connectivity Gaps** | Intermittent cellular coverage, remote rural base station outages, or offline local sync delays. | Readings queue locally on device; delayed transmission to clinic dashboard; intermittent packet loss. | `connectivity_status == "offline"` tracking; `offline_rate` component scoring in `score_reliability()`. | Linear penalty on connectivity component (15% weight); separate ablation benchmarking for online, intermittent, and offline states. | If offline rate $>30\%$, reliability score drops; actionable status requires verified sync; manual worker visit advised. | System relies on accurate local client metadata flags to distinguish network failure from non-adherence. |
| **3** | **Irregular Measurement Times** | Unscheduled measurements, varying maternal routines, morning vs evening diurnal shifts, or burst reporting. | High temporal variance distorting rate-of-change ($\text{mmHg/day}$) slope estimation in linear regression. | Coefficient of variation (CV = std / mean) of inter-reading intervals in hours; penalizes burstiness. | Regularity sub-score (20% weight); penalizes irregular timing when CV $>0.5$; default penalty (0.8) if $n < 3$. | Flags `CAUTION` with recommendation to establish a consistent morning measurement schedule before clinical escalation. | Simple linear regression does not model non-linear circadian blood pressure rhythms or diurnal variation. |
| **4** | **Missing Data** | Patient omissions, skipped days, device sync failure, or partial transmission of multi-biometric panels. | Incomplete longitudinal trend window; insufficient statistical power to confirm clinical escalation. | Missing rate computed as $1.0 - (n / \text{expected\_points})$ based on monitoring span in `check_quality()`. | Completeness sub-score (30% weight); missing rate $>35\%$ applies hard score cap ($\le 50.0$). | Hard safe fallback: trend analysis suspended; dashboard prompts health worker home verification visit. | Imputation is intentionally not performed to avoid introducing synthetic biometric artifacts into clinical records. |
| **5** | **Manual / Device Conflict** | Differing calibration between home manual cuff and automated clinic device, improper cuff size, or incorrect cuff placement. | Discrepant readings confuse clinical trend; acting on incorrect source leads to misdiagnosed preeclampsia. | Nearest-neighbor comparison within 48-hour lookback window; flags divergence $>10\%$ between sources. | Source agreement component (20% weight) drops to 30.0; hard cap applied to composite score ($\le 60.0$). | Suppresses actionable alert; triggers `CAUTION` state requiring health worker to perform side-by-side cuff calibration check. | Does not resolve which source is physiologically correct; defers determination to clinical personnel. |
| **6** | **Sensor Anomalies** | Sensor hardware glitch, motion artifact, dropped telemetry bit, or sudden cuff deflation error. | Single spurious spike (e.g. $+35\text{ mmHg}$) induces false steep regression slope, causing unnecessary hospital referral. | Consecutive-reading jump detector: flags isolated adjacent jump $>20\%$ whose magnitude $>2\times$ median of prior jumps. | Step-change anomaly flag caps composite reliability score at $\le 55.0$ and appends explicit anomaly note to rationale. | Clinical alert suppressed; `SAFE_FALLBACK` prompts immediate repeat measurement to rule out hardware failure. | Slow, gradual sensor calibration decay (sensor drift over weeks) mimics real physiological rise and cannot be detected via jump analysis. |
| **7** | **False Positives** | Corrupt telemetry, undetected artifacts, or extreme variance interpreted as genuine preeclampsia. | Unnecessary 4-hour travel to district hospital, patient anxiety, crowded triage wards, and wasted clinic resources. | Multi-factor composite reliability scoring (5 weighted dimensions) combined with strict linear regression p-value ($<0.05$) and correlation ($|r| > 0.3$). | Requires both high reliability ($\ge 70$) and high trend confidence ($\ge 60\%$) before issuing actionable alert; displays itemized harm penalties. | Non-actionable or caution status; requires secondary confirmatory reading before escalating to clinic visit. | Unavoidable boundary trade-offs near threshold cutoffs ($70.0$) may occasionally require human oversight. |
| **8** | **False Negatives** | Scorer overly conservative; genuinely deteriorating patient dismissed due to data quality penalties. | Delayed clinical intervention for preeclampsia or gestational diabetes; increased maternal-fetal morbidity. | Two-tiered decision hierarchy: cases with detected slope but low confidence are routed to `CAUTION` rather than dismissed. | `CAUTION` state enforces 24-hour mandatory re-check and community health worker dispatch instead of silent failure. | Active warning banner displayed with required follow-up timeframe (24h); harm score explicitly quantifies delayed real-action risk. | If patient completely ceases monitoring ($n < 3$), system cannot infer physiology and must rely on non-technical outreach. |
| **9** | **Uncertainty** | High statistical variance in telemetry, border-case p-values, or small sample sizes ($n < 5$). | Overconfidence in marginal trends leading to premature clinical interventions or false reassurance. | Multiplicative uncertainty quantification combining normalized reliability score ($0.0 - 1.0$) with trend sample size and p-value discount factors. | Explicit confidence percentage ($0 - 100\%$) and qualitative confidence tier displayed prominently in header metrics. | If confidence $<60\%$, actionable status is blocked even if reliability score $\ge 70$; prompts confirmatory verification reading. | Confidence metric reflects statistical fit and data integrity, not biological diagnostic certainty. |
| **10** | **Incorrect Interpretation of Unreliable Trends** | Clinician viewing visual trend graph without inspecting underlying telemetry quality flags. | Misinterpreting an artifactual regression line as physiological reality, overriding system warnings. | Header metric banners with color-coded outcomes (Green=Actionable, Yellow=Caution, Red=Safe Fallback, Blue=Not Actionable). | Visual presentation locks: trend lines accompanied by missing-gap indicators, score breakdown charts, and explicit textual evidence summaries. | Prominent warning banner and explicit recommendation: "Do NOT schedule clinic visit based on current data." | Dashboard cannot physically stop a care provider from taking unsupported actions outside the software. |
| **11** | **Simulated-Data Limitations** | Simulation models may not capture full chaotic spectrum of human behavioral and environmental variations. | Algorithms optimized on simulated noise distributions may underperform when deployed against real-world populations. | Rigorous scenario auditing across 9 distinct failure modes (outages, conflicts, spikes, jitter, noise, stale, sparse). | Benchmark evaluation clearly reports simulated provenance; synthetic distributions documented transparently in evaluation reports. | Clear disclaimer: *"These are simulated evaluation results and do not represent clinical validation."* | Real clinical validation on prospective de-identified clinic cohorts remains required prior to field deployment. |
| **12** | **Lack of Clinical Validation** | System has not completed randomized clinical trials or multi-center observational studies in remote clinics. | Unvalidated software cannot be utilized for diagnostic or prescriptive medical decisions. | Documentation, architecture, and UI explicitly define system scope as data-quality screening only. | Strict non-diagnostic framing: system evaluates *data integrity for human review*, never diagnosing maternal diseases. | Outputs always framed as recommendations for human data verification, never automated treatment or diagnosis. | Regulatory clearance (e.g. FDA SaMD / CE mark) required before any operational clinical adoption. |
| **13** | **Capacity Limitations** | Surge in remote monitoring enrollments overwhelming rural care coordinator staffing. | Review queues backlog; critical trend alerts delayed beyond the 48-hour actionable window. | `OPERATIONAL_LIMITS.max_pending_assessments` (50) enforced in `run_pipeline()`; `truncated` boolean returned. | Truncation safety flag; batch processor evaluates priority tasks up to 50 assessments and emits capacity warnings. | Safe operational fallback: unassessed cases marked as *"Pending manual/community verification"* without pretending automated completion. | Workload management relies on manual coordinator triage; no automated multi-queue scheduling engine exists. |
| **14** | **Scheduling Limitations** | Patient re-checks missed due to lack of calendar integration or automated SMS dispatch. | Recommended 24h confirmatory reading or health worker dispatch forgotten by overworked staff. | Header metric displays operational recheck interval (`24h`); decision outcome specifies explicit follow-up timeframe. | Explicit action checklist embedded in `fallback_actions` dataclass field and exported in assessment JSON audit trail. | Operational fallback: care coordinator manual daily review workflow utilizing the "All Patients" overview tab. | System does not feature an active automated scheduling daemon or two-way patient messaging gateway. |
| **15** | **Deployment / Resource Limitations** | Remote clinic workstation running on low-spec hardware (limited CPU/RAM) or intermittent power. | Software crashes, UI latency freezes, or memory leaks during batch assessment processing. | Quantitative benchmarking of process CPU time (`GetProcessTimes`), traced heap memory (`tracemalloc`), and working set RSS. | Lean dependency footprint (standard Python library + numpy, pandas, scipy, streamlit); in-memory execution $<1\text{ MB}$ traced RAM. | Fast per-profile scoring ($<100\text{ ms}$); graceful batch truncation prevents out-of-memory crashes. | True low-power Android SoC / microcontroller hardware traces not yet benchmarked; tested on desktop proxy. |

## Operational Harm Prevention Summary

The reliability scoring engine enforces harm mitigation through four mandatory safety gates:

1. **Unconditional Alert Suppression ($<50$ Reliability)**: When telemetry completeness, regularity, or quality collapses, clinical alerts are blocked regardless of mathematical slope.
2. **Mandatory Caution & Verification ($50 - 69$ Reliability)**: Trends with borderline data integrity or moderate uncertainty ($<60\%$ confidence) are routed to verification protocols rather than clinic referrals.
3. **Harm Score Display**: Downstream financial and clinical costs of false alarms (unnecessary travel, patient anxiety, clinic resource depletion) are quantified before action is taken.
4. **Transparent Evidence Audit Trail**: Every score is accompanied by raw reading IDs, component breakdown percentages, and natural-language rationales to ensure clinical supervisors can audit decisions.
